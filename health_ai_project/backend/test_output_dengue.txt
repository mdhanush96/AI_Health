🏥 Likely Condition: Dengue

Dengue is a significant global public health concern, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions, caused by the dengue virus (DENV) and transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. This mosquito-borne viral infection affects an estimated 390 million people annually, with a substantial number of cases leading to severe illness and mortality. The disease ranges from asymptomatic infection to a severe, life-threatening condition known as severe dengue or dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF). A comprehensive understanding of dengue, including its overview, symptoms, causes, treatment, and when to seek medical attention, is crucial for effective prevention and management.

Causes:
  • Viral serotypes and genotypes — The four distinct serotypes and their genetic diversity influence epidemic potential and disease severity
  • Vector ecology — Aedes aegypti mosquitoes are well-adapted to urban environments, breeding in domestic water containers, making urban areas particularly vulnerable. Climate change and urbanization expand the vector's range
  • Host immune response — Previous infection with one serotype can lead to ADE upon subsequent infection with a different serotype, increasing the risk of severe disease
  • Host factors — Age, genetic background, and comorbidities such as obesity and diabetes can influence disease severity. Individuals with compromised immune systems tend to experience more severe forms of the disease
  • Pathogenesis mechanisms — The dengue virus's interaction with the host immune system and its ability to induce plasma leakage are critical to severe disease development. The viral protein NS1 is known to disrupt endothelial junctions, contributing to capillary leakage

Common Symptoms:
  • High-grade fever, often with a biphasic pattern
  • Severe headache, particularly retro-orbital pain
  • Myalgia (muscle pain) and arthralgia (joint pain), leading to the moniker "breakbone fever"
  • Rash, which can be maculopapular or morbilliform, often appearing between days 3 and 5 of the illness
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Mild hemorrhagic manifestations, such as petechiae or gingival bleeding. A significant proportion of infections, estimated to be about 25%, are asymptomatic. However, in some cases, the disease can progress to severe dengue, characterized by specific…
  • Abdominal pain or tenderness
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Clinical fluid accumulation (e.g., ascites, pleural effusion)
  • Mucosal bleeding
  • High Fever
  • Severe Headache

Possible Complications:
  The disease ranges from asymptomatic infection to a severe, life-threatening condition known as severe dengue or dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF).

When to Seek Medical Attention:
  • Persistent high fever — lasting more than 2 days
  • Any warning signs of severe dengue — which typically appear as the fever subsides (the critical phase), usually between days 3 and 7 of illness. These include:
  • Severe abdominal pain or tenderness
  • Persistent vomiting (three or more times in an hour, or four to five times in six hours)

🏥 2. Migraine (12.2%)

Aura symptoms usually develop gradually over 5-20 minutes and last less than 60 minutes. Headache Phase: This is the most recognized phase, characterized by moderate to severe throbbing or pulsating pain, often unilateral but can be bilateral. The pain usually lasts from 4 to 72 hours if untreated. Associated symptoms are common and include nausea, vomiting, photophobia (increased sensitivity to light), and phonophobia (increased sensitivity to sound).

Common Symptoms:
