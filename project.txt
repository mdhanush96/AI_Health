SECTION 2: ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The increasing prevalence of chronic and infectious diseases worldwide demands
accessible, intelligent, and reliable healthcare advisory systems. Traditional
symptom checkers rely on rule-based decision trees or shallow keyword matching,
which lack contextual understanding and clinical reasoning capabilities. This
paper presents MedAI, a full-stack AI-powered healthcare chatbot that integrates
a fine-tuned ClinicalBERT model for symptom-based disease classification with a
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline for comprehensive medical
knowledge delivery. The system processes natural language symptom descriptions,
classifies them into one of 22 disease categories with calibrated confidence
scores, and augments predictions with semantically retrieved medical knowledge
using FAISS vector search and FLAN-T5 generative summarization. The architecture
further incorporates a two-pass symptom verification mechanism that re-ranks
ClinicalBERT predictions by matching reported symptoms against a curated
knowledge base using Jaccard similarity and synonym-aware phrase matching. The
system supports medical report summarization through multi-pass T5 question
answering, regex-based entity extraction, and SciSpacy biomedical NER. Emergency
detection for critical conditions is implemented via keyword scanning with
real-time helpline routing. The platform is deployed as a responsive web
application with a React 18 frontend and Django REST Framework backend,
supporting token-based authentication, multi-conversation persistence, and
real-time file upload processing for PDF, image, and text-based medical reports.
Experimental evaluation on the Disease and Symptoms Dataset from Kaggle
demonstrates that the proposed hybrid ClinicalBERT-RAG approach achieves
[PLACEHOLDER: accuracy]% classification accuracy across 22 disease categories,
with the symptom verification layer improving prediction relevance by
[PLACEHOLDER: improvement percentage]%. The system provides clinically
informative responses while maintaining appropriate medical disclaimers and
emergency escalation protocols.

SECTION 4: INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

4.1 Problem Statement
---------------------

The global healthcare system faces mounting pressure from increasing patient
volumes, shortage of medical professionals, and delayed access to preliminary
medical guidance. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), there is a
global deficit of approximately 18 million health workers, most acutely felt in
low- and middle-income countries. Patients frequently experience prolonged wait
times for initial consultations, during which symptoms may deteriorate and
anxiety may escalate. The need for accessible, reliable, and intelligent
preliminary medical advisory tools has become a critical area of research in
healthcare informatics.

4.2 Importance of AI Healthcare Chatbots
----------------------------------------

Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven healthcare chatbots offer a scalable
solution for preliminary symptom assessment and health information delivery.
These systems leverage Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Machine Learning
(ML) to interpret user-described symptoms and provide disease predictions,
medical knowledge, and actionable health guidance. Unlike traditional
rule-based symptom checkers that rely on rigid decision trees, modern NLP-based
chatbots can understand contextual symptom descriptions, handle linguistic
variations, and provide nuanced medical information grounded in clinical
knowledge bases.

4.3 Limitations of Existing Systems
------------------------------------

Current healthcare chatbot implementations exhibit several limitations:

    (a) Rule-Based Approaches: Systems such as Ada Health and Buoy Health
        employ decision-tree algorithms that require exhaustive manual
        encoding of symptom-disease mappings, resulting in limited
        scalability and inability to handle novel symptom descriptions.

    (b) Shallow ML Models: Traditional machine learning classifiers
        (Naive Bayes, SVM, Random Forest) applied to symptom classification
        lack the ability to capture semantic relationships between symptoms,
        resulting in degraded performance for ambiguous or multi-symptom
        presentations.

    (c) Lack of Contextual Knowledge Delivery: Most existing systems
        provide a disease label without comprehensive medical context,
        leaving users without understanding of causes, treatment options,
        complications, or when to seek emergency care.

    (d) No Report Processing Capability: Existing chatbots typically
        cannot process uploaded medical reports (PDF, images) for
        automated summarization and entity extraction.

    (e) Absence of Post-Classification Verification: Predictions from
        classification models are presented without validation against
        curated clinical knowledge, leading to potential misalignment
        between predicted diseases and reported symptoms.

4.4 Motivation
--------------

The convergence of transformer-based language models (BERT, GPT), dense
retrieval systems (FAISS), and instruction-tuned generative models (FLAN-T5)
presents an opportunity to develop healthcare chatbots that combine the
classification precision of fine-tuned clinical models with the knowledge
depth of retrieval-augmented generation. This research is motivated by the
need to bridge the gap between accurate disease prediction and
comprehensive, contextually grounded medical information delivery.

4.5 Research Contributions
--------------------------

The principal contributions of this work are as follows:

    1. A hybrid disease prediction framework combining fine-tuned
       ClinicalBERT classification with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation
       (RAG) pipeline using FAISS vector search and FLAN-T5 generation
       for 22 disease categories.

    2. A two-pass symptom verification mechanism that re-ranks
       ClinicalBERT predictions using Jaccard similarity, synonym-aware
       phrase matching, and weighted score fusion (60% model confidence,
       40% symptom match).

    3. A multi-modal medical report summarization engine supporting PDF,
       image (OCR), and text input formats with multi-pass T5 question
       answering, regex-based entity extraction, and SciSpacy biomedical
       NER.

    4. An emergency detection system with keyword-based critical condition
       identification and real-time helpline routing.

    5. A production-ready full-stack deployment architecture with React 18
       frontend, Django REST Framework backend, token-based authentication,
       multi-conversation management, and GPU-accelerated inference.

5.2 Research Gap
----------------

The literature reveals a significant gap between classification-only systems
that provide disease predictions without contextual knowledge and large
language model systems that generate fluent but potentially hallucinated
medical content. The proposed MedAI system addresses this gap by:

    (a) Combining fine-tuned ClinicalBERT classification with grounded
        knowledge retrieval, ensuring predictions are both accurate and
        contextually supported.

    (b) Implementing a two-pass symptom verification layer that validates
        model predictions against curated clinical knowledge.

    (c) Using FAISS vector search with FLAN-T5 generation to produce
        factually grounded medical explanations rather than relying on
        unconstrained text generation.

6.2 Data Preprocessing
-----------------------

The data preprocessing pipeline consists of the following stages:

    (a) Text Sanitization:
        - Input validation enforcing 3–5000 character length constraints
        - Removal of special characters and extraneous whitespace
        - UTF-8 encoding normalization

    (b) Tokenization:
        - BertTokenizer with ClinicalBERT vocabulary (28,996 tokens)
        - Maximum sequence length: 128 tokens
        - Padding and truncation applied uniformly
        - Special token insertion: [CLS] for classification, [SEP] for
          segment separation

    (c) Multi-Format Input Processing:
        - Plain text: Direct UTF-8 decoding
        - PDF documents: pdfplumber extraction with PyPDF2 fallback;
          includes table extraction support
        - Images (PNG, JPG, JPEG): EasyOCR primary extraction with
          pytesseract fallback for OCR-based text extraction
        - File size validation: Maximum 10 MB per upload

    (d) Medical Entity Recognition (Preprocessing):
        - SciSpacy biomedical NER model applied to extract medical
          entities categorized as: diseases, medications, symptoms,
          tests, procedures, and anatomy
        - Drug classification via pharmaceutical suffix matching
          (-statin, -pril, -sartan, -olol, -azole, -mycin)
        - Noise filtering using a blacklist of ~60 common non-medical
          terms (patient, demographics, age, history, normal, etc.)


6.3 Symptom Classification Model (ClinicalBERT)
------------------------------------------------

    Architecture    : BertForSequenceClassification
    Base Model      : ClinicalBERT (pre-trained on clinical notes from
                      MIMIC-III corpus)
    Hidden Size     : 768
    Attention Heads : 12
    Transformer Layers : 12
    Vocabulary Size : 28,996
    Max Position Embeddings : 512
    Number of Output Classes : 22 (disease categories)
    Classification Head : Linear layer (768 → 22) with softmax activation

    The ClinicalBERT model, originally pre-trained on approximately 2 million
    clinical notes from the MIMIC-III database, was fine-tuned on the Disease
    and Symptoms Dataset for multi-class disease classification. The model
    accepts tokenized symptom descriptions as input and produces probability
    distributions over the 22 disease classes. The top-k (default k=3)
    predictions are returned with associated confidence scores.

    Model Loading Strategy:
        - Thread-safe singleton pattern with double-check locking
        - Automatic GPU/CPU device detection with CUDA support
        - Optional float16 half-precision for reduced memory footprint
        - Environment-configurable forced CPU mode (MEDAI_FORCE_CPU)

    Risk Classification:
        - confidence >= 75%  → "High Probability"
        - 50% <= confidence < 75%  → "Moderate Probability"
        - confidence < 50%  → "Low Probability"


6.4 Symptom Verification (Two-Pass Verification)
-------------------------------------------------

A post-classification symptom verification layer re-ranks ClinicalBERT
predictions by computing symptom overlap between the user's reported
symptoms and the known symptom profile of each predicted disease from
the knowledge base.

    Scoring Formula:
        combined_score = 0.60 × model_confidence + 0.40 × symptom_match_score

    Symptom Matching Algorithms:
        (a) Word-Level Overlap: Jaccard similarity between tokenized
            user input and knowledge base symptom descriptions,
            with stop-word filtering.
        (b) Phrase-Level Partial Matching: Substring matching for
            multi-word symptom phrases.
        (c) Direct Key Symptom Matching: Exact substring matching
            against disease-specific key symptoms.
        (d) Synonym Expansion: 33 synonym groups covering medical
            terminology variations (e.g., heartburn ↔ acid reflux,
            chest pain ↔ thoracic pain, rash ↔ skin eruption).

    Weak Match Threshold: 0.15 (below this threshold, no single
    disease is considered a strong match, and the system communicates
    uncertainty to the user).


6.5 Knowledge Retrieval (RAG Pipeline)
--------------------------------------

The Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline augments ClinicalBERT
predictions with comprehensive medical knowledge:

    6.5.1 Embedding Model
        - Model: sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2
        - Embedding Dimension: 384
        - Pooling: Mean pooling with L2 normalization
        - Purpose: Converts symptom queries and knowledge chunks into
          dense vector representations

    6.5.2 Vector Store (FAISS)
        - Library: Facebook AI Similarity Search (FAISS)
        - Index Type: Flat L2 (exact nearest neighbor)
        - Top-K Retrieval: k=10 (configurable)
        - Distance Threshold: 2.0 (chunks with distance > 2.0 filtered)
        - Metadata: Pickled alongside FAISS index with disease labels
          and source text

    6.5.3 Knowledge Base
        - Format: 22 JSON files, one per disease
        - Sections per disease: Overview, Symptoms, Causes, Treatment,
          When to See a Doctor, Prevention/Complications
        - Two-tier structure:
          (i)  disease_info.json: Compact reference (description,
               common symptoms, advice) for quick lookup
          (ii) Per-disease JSON files: Comprehensive medical knowledge
               for each condition

    6.5.4 Generative Model (FLAN-T5)
        - Model: google/flan-t5-base
        - Max Input Length: 1,024 tokens
        - Max Output Length: 512 tokens
        - Temperature: 0.7
        - Generation Strategy: Configurable (sampling vs. beam search)
        - Prompt Template: Instruction-tuned prompt with disease context
          requesting coverage of: condition explanation, causes/risk
          factors, key symptoms, complications, lifestyle/dietary
          modifications, and emergency indicators

    6.5.5 Response Caching
        - Mechanism: Thread-safe in-memory LRU cache
        - Key: SHA-256 hash of input text
        - TTL: 300 seconds (configurable)
        - Purpose: Reduces redundant inference for identical queries


6.6 Medical Report Summarization
---------------------------------

The system supports multi-format medical report summarization through
a multi-pass extraction pipeline:

    Pass 1 — FLAN-T5 Question Answering:
        Five focused queries are applied to the extracted report text:
        (a) Patient details (name, age, gender, date, patient ID)
        (b) Diagnoses identified in the report
        (c) Test results and clinical findings
        (d) Medications prescribed with dosages
        (e) Recommendations and follow-up instructions

    Pass 2 — Regex-Based Extraction:
        Pattern matching for structured data:
        (a) Patient demographics (name patterns, age, gender, date, ID)
        (b) Diagnoses (section headers + disease pattern matching)
        (c) Medications (drug patterns with dosage and frequency)
        (d) Lab values (40+ test patterns: hemoglobin, glucose,
            cholesterol, TSH, creatinine, platelet count, etc.)
        (e) Recommendations (advice/follow-up section parsing)

    Pass 3 — Biomedical NER:
        SciSpacy model applied for entity extraction across categories:
        diseases, medications, symptoms, tests, procedures, anatomy

    Output Merging:
        Results from all three passes are merged and deduplicated to
        produce a structured summary containing: patient information,
        diagnoses, medications, lab values, recommendations, and
        extracted medical entities.


6.7 Emergency Detection
------------------------

A keyword-based emergency detection module scans user input for 12
critical medical keywords:

    Keywords: chest pain, difficulty breathing, seizure, unconscious,
    severe bleeding, stroke symptoms, heart attack, anaphylaxis,
    choking, poisoning, severe burn, suicidal thoughts

    Upon detection, the system:
        (a) Flags the response with an emergency alert
        (b) Provides a conversational explanation of the detected
            emergency condition
        (c) Returns emergency helpline numbers:
            - 112 (National Emergency)
            - 108 (Ambulance)
            - 102 (Women & Children)
            - iCall Helpline
            - Vandrevala Foundation Helpline


6.8 System Architecture
------------------------

The complete system architecture consists of the following layers:

    Layer 1: Presentation Layer (Frontend)
        - Framework: React 18 with React Router v7
        - UI: ChatGPT-style multi-conversation interface
        - Features: Real-time messaging, file upload, conversation
          persistence (localStorage), user profiles, authentication
        - Communication: Axios HTTP client (120s timeout for RAG)

    Layer 2: API Layer (Backend)
        - Framework: Django 5.x with Django REST Framework
        - Authentication: Token-based (DRF TokenAuthentication)
        - Endpoints: 8 REST API endpoints (predict, predict-rag,
          health, history, register, login, logout, profile)
        - CORS: Configured for cross-origin frontend communication

    Layer 3: ML Inference Layer
        - ClinicalBERT: Disease classification (22 classes)
        - Symptom Verifier: Two-pass re-ranking
        - RAG Pipeline: FAISS retrieval + FLAN-T5 generation
        - Report Summarizer: Multi-pass extraction
        - Medical NER: SciSpacy entity recognition
        - Emergency Detector: Keyword-based safety layer

    Layer 4: Data Layer
        - Database: MySQL 8.x (user profiles, prediction logs)
        - Knowledge Base: 22 JSON disease files
        - Vector Store: Pre-built FAISS index (384-dim embeddings)
        - Model Weights: Fine-tuned ClinicalBERT (model.safetensors)

SECTION 9: DISCUSSION
================================================================================

9.1 Advantages of the Proposed System
--------------------------------------

    (a) Hybrid Architecture: The combination of ClinicalBERT classification
        with RAG-based knowledge retrieval provides both accurate disease
        prediction and comprehensive medical context, addressing the key
        limitation of classification-only systems that offer disease labels
        without informative medical guidance.

    (b) Two-Pass Symptom Verification: The post-classification verification
        mechanism using synonym-aware Jaccard similarity re-ranking ensures
        that predictions are clinically consistent with reported symptoms,
        reducing false positives and improving user trust.

    (c) Grounded Generation: Unlike unconstrained LLM generation (e.g.,
        ChatGPT, ChatDoctor) that may produce hallucinated medical content,
        the FLAN-T5 generation is constrained to FAISS-retrieved chunks
        from a curated knowledge base, ensuring factual grounding.

    (d) Multi-Modal Input: Support for text, PDF, and image (OCR) input
        formats enables comprehensive medical report processing, extending
        the system's utility beyond symptom description.

    (e) Emergency Safety Layer: Real-time detection of 12 critical medical
        conditions with emergency helpline routing provides an essential
        safety mechanism absent in most commercial healthcare chatbots.

    (f) Production-Ready Deployment: The full-stack architecture with
        token-based authentication, multi-conversation persistence, GPU
        acceleration, response caching, and thread-safe model loading
        demonstrates deployment readiness beyond academic prototypes.

    (g) Responsible AI Design: Medical disclaimers are embedded in every
        response, the system explicitly states it is not a replacement for
        professional medical consultation, and emergency escalation is
        automatic and prominent.

9.2 Limitations
----------------

    (a) Dataset Scope: The system is limited to 22 disease categories from
        the Disease and Symptoms Dataset. Diseases outside this taxonomy
        cannot be predicted, and the system may force-classify symptoms of
        unlisted diseases into the closest available category.

    (b) Language Limitation: The system currently processes only English
        language input. Patients communicating in regional or non-English
        languages require translation before using the system.

    (c) No Temporal Reasoning: The system treats each symptom description
        as an independent query without tracking symptom progression over
        time, limiting its ability to detect deteriorating conditions.

    (d) Knowledge Base Currency: The JSON-based knowledge base requires
        manual updates to incorporate new medical findings, treatment
        protocols, or emerging diseases.

    (e) No Clinical Validation: The system has not undergone clinical
        trials or validation by healthcare professionals against
        established diagnostic protocols.

    (f) FLAN-T5 Generation Quality: As a base-size model, FLAN-T5
        may produce inconsistent or repetitive explanations for complex
        medical conditions compared to larger generative models.

    (g) OCR Accuracy: Medical report text extraction from images depends
        on OCR quality, which may degrade with handwritten prescriptions,
        poor image quality, or non-standard document formats.

SECTION 10: CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper presented MedAI, an intelligent healthcare chatbot that integrates
fine-tuned ClinicalBERT classification with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation
(RAG) pipeline for symptom-based disease prediction and contextualized medical
knowledge delivery. The system addresses critical limitations of existing
healthcare chatbots by combining classification precision with grounded
knowledge generation, two-pass symptom verification, multi-modal medical report
summarization, and real-time emergency detection.

The proposed architecture demonstrates that hybrid approaches — combining
domain-specific transformer models with dense retrieval and instruction-tuned
generation — can provide clinically relevant, factually grounded, and
comprehensive medical guidance while maintaining responsible AI guardrails
including medical disclaimers and emergency escalation protocols.

The ClinicalBERT model, fine-tuned on the Disease and Symptoms Dataset,
achieves [PLACEHOLDER: accuracy]% classification accuracy across 22 disease
categories, with the symptom verification layer improving prediction relevance
through weighted score fusion. The FAISS-based retrieval system ensures that
generative responses are constrained to curated medical knowledge, mitigating
the hallucination risks inherent in unconstrained large language models.

Future work includes:

    (a) Expanding the disease taxonomy beyond 22 categories through
        additional dataset integration and model retraining.

    (b) Implementing multi-turn dialogue management for symptom
        elicitation and progressive differential diagnosis.

    (c) Integrating multilingual support for non-English speaking
        patient populations.

    (d) Conducting clinical validation studies with healthcare
        professionals to assess diagnostic concordance.

    (e) Implementing federated learning for privacy-preserving model
        improvement from distributed clinical data.

    (f) Replacing FLAN-T5-base with larger instruction-tuned models
        (FLAN-T5-large, Mistral-7B) for improved generation quality.

    (g) Adding temporal symptom tracking for longitudinal health
        monitoring and disease progression detection.
